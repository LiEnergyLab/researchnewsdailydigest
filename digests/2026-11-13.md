### [Multiphysics Modeling of Coupled Multiphase Transport and Electrocatalysis in Industrial Zero-Gap Alkaline Water Electrolyzers: Cell Size and Flow Field Effects](https://doi.org/10.3390/catal16111001)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** alkaline electrolyzer modeling · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-11-11 · **Authors:** Bin Huang, Lei Bao, Qi Chen, Jiwen Peng, Zhuo Li, Huanhuan Li

A validated multiphysics model of industrial zero-gap alkaline electrolyzers (mean error under 3% against a 5 MW system) shows larger-diameter cells achieve higher electrochemical efficiency at fixed current density due to better bubble removal, and that anode support-mesh permeability anisotropy is non-negligible. ### [Interfacial Stability Engineering of Proton Exchange Membrane Water Electrolyzer Anodes](https://doi.org/10.1002/adfm.79548)

This review argues that PEMWE anode durability is governed by multilevel interfacial stability rather than intrinsic catalyst activity alone, framing degradation as the near-surface stability window of Ir/Ru sites being reshaped by catalyst-layer percolation, ionomer distribution, pore architecture, PTL contact, membrane contamination, and metal migration under dynamic operation. ### [Impact of Operating Conditions on Degradation Behavior of a 500 kW-Class PEM Water Electrolysis System](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhydene.2026.158416)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** PEMWE degradation · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-11-10 · **Authors:** Kensaku Nagasawa, Hirokazu Kojima, Yuki Tsuda, Tetsuhiko Maeda

A 500 kW-class PEM electrolyzer degraded at 74 μV/h under daily start-stop operation but 262 μV/h under accelerated durability testing, indicating that dynamic cycling drives degradation more than current load does. Impedance data attribute most of the loss to rising internal resistance, with charge-transfer resistance growing only in prolonged stress testing. The authors also report a reproducible linear correlation between initial cell voltage and subsequent voltage rise, useful for lifetime prediction.

Square-wave potential modulation of iridium oxide anodes in acid boosts OER activity by up to 480% versus static operation. ### [Free Ionomer Controls Adsorption in Electrolyzer Inks](https://doi.org/10.1149/1945-7111/aeb7d2)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** PEM electrolyzer ink · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-22 · **Authors:** Maximilian Degirmenci, Siddharth Rajupet, Diego Soestrino, Sunilkumar Khandavalli, J Park, Luis Perales

PFSA ionomer adsorption on IrOx in electrolyzer inks depends on the free-ionomer concentration and solvent composition, not on catalyst loading or the ionomer-to-catalyst ratio. The I:C ratio is a poor predictor; free-ionomer weight percent collapses adsorption onto a single isotherm, with electrostatic repulsion limiting uptake in water-rich solvents and solvation/coverage effects dominating in alcohol-rich ones. The authors also link these ink parameters to sedimentation, X-ray scattering, and single-cell performance.

### [Unraveling Water Balances behind Salt Formation in CO2 Reduction Electrolyzers](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c12691)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electrolyzer salt · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-23 · **Authors:** Yuge Feng, Shaoyun Hao, Zhou Yu, Ziteng Zuo, Tae‐Ung Wi, Ahmad Elgazzar

A quantitative water balance across the cathode chamber shows that inlet CO₂ from a water bubbler is often undersaturated, so evaporation inside the cell lowers the local liquid water-to-K⁺ ratio enough to precipitate bicarbonate salts—even when anolyte crossover supplies nominally sufficient water. Over-humidifying suppresses salt formation but causes electrode flooding, so the work frames water management as a trade-off between dehydration-driven crystallization and flooding-limited transport.

A free-standing anion exchange membrane made of 90 wt% layered double hydroxide in a fibrillated PTFE scaffold shows zero swelling and 0.089 Ω cm² area-specific resistance, and it survived 1,344 h in 1 M and 30 wt% KOH at 80 °C where commercial polymer membranes fail quickly. ### [Beyond Faradaic Efficiency: Towards efficient electroreduction of CO2 in Membrane-Electrode assemblies](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mattod.2026.103537)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2RR MEA · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-27 · **Authors:** Huali Wu, Yan Jiao, Damien Voiry

This review argues that scaling CO2 electroreduction in membrane-electrode assemblies is a multi-physics problem—coupling compression, mass transport, and thermal management—rather than simple geometric enlargement.
