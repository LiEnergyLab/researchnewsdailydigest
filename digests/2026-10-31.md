PFSA ionomer adsorption on IrOx in electrolyzer inks depends on the free-ionomer concentration and solvent composition, not on catalyst loading or the ionomer-to-catalyst ratio. ### [Unraveling Water Balances behind Salt Formation in CO2 Reduction Electrolyzers](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c12691)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electrolyzer salt · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-23 · **Authors:** Yuge Feng, Shaoyun Hao, Zhou Yu, Ziteng Zuo, Tae‐Ung Wi, Ahmad Elgazzar

A quantitative water balance for CO2 electrolyzer cathodes shows that inlet CO2 from a water bubbler is often not fully humidified, so evaporation inside the cathode chamber lowers the local liquid-water-to-K+ ratio and drives bicarbonate salt precipitation—even when anolyte crossover water would otherwise suffice to dissolve the K+. Excessive humidification stops salt formation but causes electrode flooding, so water management must be tuned between these two failure modes.
