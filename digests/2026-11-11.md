### [Water-Transport Resistance of Perfluorosulfonic-Acid (PFSA) Membranes: Delineating the Role of Interface and Chemistry](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsaem.6c02544)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** PEM membrane transport · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-11-02 · **Authors:** Catherine M. Weiss, Shouwen Shi, Adam Z. Weber, Ahmet Kusoglu

Interfacial and bulk water-transport resistances in PFSA membranes are governed by distinct mechanisms, with interfacial resistance dropping as side-chain length increases while bulk resistance is lowest for medium side-chain lengths (3M). Water content is the dominant controlling variable—far more influential than temperature—and interfacial transport requires substantially more thermal energy to overcome than bulk transport. The results suggest side-chain chemistry can be tuned to balance water transport against proton conductivity, though the structure–transport relationship is not monotonic.

PFSA ionomer adsorption onto IrOx in electrolyzer inks depends on the free-ionomer concentration and solvent composition, not on catalyst loading or the ionomer-to-catalyst ratio. ### [Speciation-Resolved Mechanistic Insights into Amine Degradation in PEI-based Sorbents Across a Wide Range of Humidities](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccst.2026.100717)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** DAC sorbent degradation · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-11-01 · **Authors:** Jiaqi Zhang, Sichi Li, Simon H. Pang, Carsten Sievers, Christopher W. Jones

Water's effect on oxidative degradation of PEI/SBA-15 at 110 °C is non-monotonic: CO₂ capacity loss is minimized at an intermediate humidity (~6 kPa H₂O) and rises sharply above it, with the product distribution shifting toward carboxylic acids and aliphatic hydroxyls at high humidity. The authors attribute the low-humidity benefit to modest polymer plasticization improving amine accessibility, and introduce a derivatization-based workflow that sorts degradation products into carbonyl, imine, hydroxyl, and carboxylic acid pools. The result defines a humidity window for nominally minimized degradation under these specific aging conditions.

### [Unraveling Water Balances behind Salt Formation in CO2 Reduction Electrolyzers](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c12691)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electrolyzer salt · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-23 · **Authors:** Yuge Feng, Shaoyun Hao, Zhou Yu, Ziteng Zuo, Tae‐Ung Wi, Ahmad Elgazzar

A quantitative water balance across all cathode-chamber sources and phases shows that salt precipitation in CO2 electrolyzers is driven mainly by evaporation: inlet CO2 through a bubbler is often not fully humidified, so liquid water evaporates and lowers the local water-to-K+ ratio below what's needed to keep cotransported K+ dissolved. Crossover water alone would suffice to dissolve the potassium, but evaporation undercuts it; conversely, over-humidification prevents salt but causes flooding, a direct trade-off. The finding frames water management as the governing lever for salt control, though the guidance remains qualitative.

### [Dynamic Electrocatalysis Enhances the Oxygen Evolution Reaction Activity of Iridium Oxides](https://doi.org/10.1002/celc.70341)

Square-wave potential modulation of iridium oxide anodes in acid raised OER activity by up to 480% versus static operation.
