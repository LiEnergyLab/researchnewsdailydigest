### [Hydrogen crossover in proton exchange membrane electrolysis cells under assembly compression: a one-dimensional multiphysics model and parametric structural design](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2026.133588)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** PEM electrolysis · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-17 · **Authors:** Jianfei Zhang, Ziqian Zhu, Jianlin Fu, Zhiguo Qu

A 1D multiphysics model shows that concentration-driven diffusion accounts for over 90% of hydrogen crossover in PEM electrolysis cells across 0–3.6 A/cm², and that assembly compression and operating temperature synergistically worsen crossover even as higher compression lowers contact resistance. A cathode design with graded porosity (0.7 in the porous transport layer, 0.3 in the catalyst layer) cut hydrogen-in-oxygen concentration by 46.5% at high current, while a hydrophobic-PTL/hydrophilic-catalyst layering raised average membrane water content by 4.69%. The work is modeling-only, so the design gains await experimental validation.

### [Water Activation as a Limiting Factor for High Energy Efficiency in CO2 Electroreduction](https://doi.org/10.1021/acselectrochem.6c00222)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electroreduction · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-09-29 · **Authors:** José Guillermo Rivera de la Cruz, Jonathan Raisin, Marc Fontecave, Dong Un Lee, Thomas Francisco Jaramillo, Tiras Y. Lin

Interfacial proton depletion under industrial current densities imposes a net thermodynamic voltage penalty of ~0.6 V in zero-gap MEA CO2 electrolyzers, setting a practical energy floor for direct C2+ production even after accounting for the Nernstian pH-gradient offset. The authors argue this penalty can only be overcome by synchronizing interfacial proton delivery with dimerization kinetics, a link not yet demonstrated at high rates in acidic CO reduction. Until then, they conclude a sequential CO2-to-CO then CO-to-C2+ cascade is the more robust industrial route.
