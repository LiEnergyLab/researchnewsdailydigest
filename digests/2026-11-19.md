### [Water-Transport Resistance of Perfluorosulfonic-Acid (PFSA) Membranes: Delineating the Role of Interface and Chemistry](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsaem.6c02544)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** PEM-water-transport · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-11-02 · **Authors:** Catherine M. Weiss, Shouwen Shi, Adam Z. Weber, Ahmet Kusoglu

Interfacial and bulk water-transport resistances in PFSA membranes are governed by distinct mechanisms: interfacial resistance falls as side-chain length increases, while bulk resistance is lowest for the medium side-chain chemistry (3M), so no single side-chain length optimizes both. Water content is the dominant controlling variable, with RH having roughly an order of magnitude more impact than temperature, and interfacial transport requires substantially more thermal energy to overcome than bulk transport. The authors build this on a systematic 12 chemistry-thickness × 16 condition dataset across Nafion, 3M, and Aquivion.

### [Unraveling Water Balances behind Salt Formation in CO2 Reduction Electrolyzers](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c12691)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electrolyzer salt · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-23 · **Authors:** Yuge Feng, Shaoyun Hao, Zhou Yu, Ziteng Zuo, Tae‐Ung Wi, Ahmad Elgazzar

A quantitative water balance across all phases shows that CO2 feed through a bubbler is often not fully humidified, so evaporation inside the cathode chamber lowers the local liquid water-to-K+ ratio and drives bicarbonate salt precipitation—even though anolyte crossover water would otherwise suffice to dissolve the cotransported K+. Over-humidifying suppresses salt formation but causes electrode flooding, indicating a trade-off between dehydration-induced crystallization and flooding-limited mass transport.

### [Three-Dimensionally Fibrillated Inorganic Composite Anion Exchange Membranes for Ampere-Scale Hydrogen Production](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02666)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** AEM water electrolysis · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-22 · **Authors:** Mengjie Zhang, Qi Kong, Jing Hu, Shuhao Wang, Mengmeng Yang, Ibrahem O. Baibars

A free-standing anion exchange membrane made of 90 wt% layered double hydroxide in a fibrillated PTFE scaffold shows zero swelling and 0.089 Ω cm² area-specific resistance, and it survived 1,344 h in 1 M and 30 wt% KOH at 80 °C where commercial polymer membranes fail quickly. The trade-off to watch is mechanical robustness of a 90 wt% inorganic, PTFE-fibrillated sheet at scale, which the abstract does not address.
