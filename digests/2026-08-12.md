### [Green Hydrogen Production Using Low-Cost Alkaline Electrolysis Powered by Renewable Energy](https://doi.org/10.2118/234800-ms)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** Alkaline electrolysis · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-08-10 · **Authors:** Ogechi Goodness Ibelegbu, Nkemakolam Chinedu Izuwa, Humphery Nwenenda Dike, Placid Ikechukwu Anyanwu, Odion Beston Akhabue, Ekwebelem Emmanuel Chinazor

In a lab-scale alkaline electrolyzer using KOH electrolyte and stainless-steel electrodes, hydrogen production only began at electrolyte concentrations of 400 g/L or higher, with the best yield (2.59 L) and Faradaic efficiency (61.9%) achieved at 600 g/L and 12 V. However, operation at 400 g/L and 12 V caused a severe efficiency collapse to 1.62% due to Joule heating and gas-bubble shielding, while the stainless-steel electrodes showed no visible wear. The results confirm that optimizing KOH concentration is critical for low-cost, decentralized hydrogen generation, but also highlight a narrow operational window where performance can degrade sharply.

Copper catalysts in flow-cell CO₂ electrolyzers can reduce CO₂ via the Eley–Rideal mechanism, where gaseous CO reacts directly with an adsorbed intermediate, rather than only through the traditionally assumed Langmuir–Hinshelwood pathway.
