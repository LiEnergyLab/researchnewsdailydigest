### [Controlled Acidity Gradients Enable CO2 Reduction to Formic Acid (Not Formate) by Molecular Electrocatalysts](https://doi.org/10.17615/031z-tg98)

This work demonstrates a dual-electrolyte cell with a proton-exchange membrane that creates controlled acidity gradients, enabling molecular catalysts to convert CO2 directly into formic acid instead of the usual formate salt. ### [In-situ soft X-ray spectroscopy of OER metal oxide electrocatalysts for water splitting](https://doi.org/10.48683/1926.00130487>)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** OER electrocatalysts · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2027-01-01 · **Authors:** James J. C. Counter

Two new working electrode assemblies enable operando XPS and NEXAFS of OER catalysts, revealing that amorphous and semi-crystalline iridium oxides form mixed Ir⁴⁺/Ir⁵⁺ states with significant oxyl species, plus previously unreported Ir 5d electronic changes. A potential-programmed NEXAFS method further resolves distinct μ₁-, μ₂-, and μ₃-O species and an oxygen redox process that forms electrophilic oxygen without oxidizing Ir⁵⁺, while aging studies show increased Ir³⁺ and structural degradation. The Au/PCTE assembly also provides the first in-situ XPS/NEXAFS of mixed Ru–Ir oxides, showing potential-dependent ruthenium segregation.

### [Assessment of degradation in Ion pair membrane high temperature PEM fuel cells using commercial and customized catalysts under varied operating conditions](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nxener.2026.100848)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** HT-PEMFC degradation · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-08-05 · **Authors:** Mengfan Zhou, Fan Zhou, Rajan Maurya, Raghunandan Sharma, Na Li, Samuel  Simon Araya

In ion-pair-membrane high-temperature PEM fuel cells, recycled platinum catalysts match or slightly beat fresh commercial catalysts under steady-state operation (degradation of −21 vs −24 µV/h), but they degrade far faster under start-stop cycling (−236 to −656 µV/h vs −75 to −370 µV/h). C–C coupling during CO₂ reduction on Cu is driven by a small population of highly active minority sites with strong CO affinity, not by the majority of surface Cu atoms, which are relatively inert. Adding secondary metals (Ag, Au, Pd, Zn) to Cu nanocatalysts strongly alters how much Cu oxide forms under applied potential pulses, and this directly controls the ethanol-to-ethylene product ratio. ### [Role of Electrolyte Concentration in Bicarbonate Electroreduction](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.6c01592)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** bicarbonate electroreduction · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-07-27 · **Authors:** Jiajie Hou, Yunzhou Wen, Leon Jacobse, Arno Bergmann, Beatriz Roldán Cuenya

In bicarbonate electroreduction on copper, catalytic activity peaks at a moderate KHCO₃ concentration because dilute solutions limit CO₂ supply while concentrated ones let (bi)carbonate ions block active sites, suppressing CO adsorption and C–C coupling. This volcano-shaped dependence, confirmed via operando Raman in a custom MEA cell, means electrolyte concentration is a direct tuning knob for optimizing integrated CO₂ capture-to-fuel systems.
