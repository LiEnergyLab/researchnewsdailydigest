### [Interfacial engineering for redirecting CO 2 electroreduction selectivity on boron-doped diamond: from formic acid to carbon monoxide](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6fd00071a)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2RR-BDD · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2027-01-01 · **Authors:** Takashi Yamamoto, Taiga Ozawa, Mai Tomisaki, Yasuaki Einaga

Using a 0.1 M KClO₄ electrolyte instead of surface modification proved the simpler route to switch boron-doped diamond's CO₂ reduction selectivity from formic acid to CO, reaching 72% Faradaic efficiency at −1.46 V vs RHE with 500 mL min⁻¹ flow at 5 °C. ### [Water-Transport Resistance of Perfluorosulfonic-Acid (PFSA) Membranes: Delineating the Role of Interface and Chemistry](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsaem.6c02544)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** PEM membrane transport · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-11-02 · **Authors:** Catherine M. Weiss, Shouwen Shi, Adam Z. Weber, Ahmet Kusoglu

Interfacial and bulk water-transport resistances in PFSA membranes are governed by distinct mechanisms: interfacial resistance drops as side-chain length increases, while bulk resistance is lowest for the medium side-chain ionomer (3M), so no single chemistry minimizes both. Water content is the dominant controlling variable, with RH having roughly an order of magnitude more impact than temperature, and interfacial transport requires substantially higher activation energy than bulk transport. The results give a mechanistic basis for tuning side-chain chemistry against the water-transport/proton-conductivity trade-off.

In a 1–20 bar three-electrode alkaline cell, raising pressure increased OER current mainly by mitigating bubble-related (non-kinetic) losses, with the enhancement scaling linearly with 1/p and leveling off at higher pressures.
