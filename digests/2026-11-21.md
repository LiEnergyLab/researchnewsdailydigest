### [Effects of pressure on the oxygen evolution reaction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.elecom.2026.108280)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** OER pressure electrolysis · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-11-01 · **Authors:** Onno van der Heijden, Aleksandr Bashkatov, Alisson H.M. da Silva, Rafaël E. Vos, M. Albers, Detlef Lohse

Operating a three-electrode alkaline cell at 1–20 bar increases OER current under transient (cyclic voltammetry) conditions mainly by mitigating bubble-related non-kinetic effects, with the enhancement scaling linearly with 1/p (i.e., with volumetric gas flux) and leveling off at higher pressure. Under chronoamperometry, higher pressure suppresses current oscillations from large-bubble release while sustaining higher current, though this steady-state gain is not fully explained by gas-flux differences alone—pointing to a pressure effect on the bubble departure mechanism. The practical takeaway: part of the reported OER improvement at pressure is a bubble-removal artifact, so cell-level pressure studies should separate mass-transport/bubble effects from intrinsic kinetics.

### [Unraveling Water Balances behind Salt Formation in CO2 Reduction Electrolyzers](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c12691)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electrolyzer salt · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-23 · **Authors:** Yuge Feng, Shaoyun Hao, Zhou Yu, Ziteng Zuo, Tae‐Ung Wi, Ahmad Elgazzar

A quantitative water balance across all cathode-chamber sources and phases shows that salt precipitation in CO2 electrolyzers is driven mainly by evaporation: inlet CO2 through a bubbler is often not fully humidified, so liquid water evaporates and lowers the local water-to-K+ ratio below what's needed to keep cotransported K+ dissolved. Crossover water alone would suffice to dissolve the potassium, but evaporation tips the balance toward bicarbonate/carbonate precipitation. Over-humidifying prevents salt but causes electrode flooding, so the work frames water management as a trade-off between dehydration-driven crystallization and flooding-limited transport.
