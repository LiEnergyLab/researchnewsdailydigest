### [Crossover breakdown analysis: Impact of mass and charge transport processes on H2 crossover in PEM water electrolysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhydene.2026.158258)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** PEM electrolysis H2 crossover · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-22 · **Authors:** Lucas Anschütz, Michel Suermann, Boris Bensmann, Patrick Trinke, Richard Hanke‐Rauschenbach

A 1D coupled mass/charge-transport model shows that H2 crossover in PEM water electrolysis rises with current density mainly because dissolved H2 transfers into cathode catalyst layer pores, supersaturating the ionomer; pore transport of H2 adds a second, smaller increase via local gas pressure. Electro-osmotic drag convection actually reduces net crossover below the zero-current diffusion baseline. The dominant mechanism weakens with thicker catalyst layers while the pore-transport contribution grows, so CCL design trades off between them.

### [Support Protection Mechanism Enables Efficient and Stable Anion Exchange Membrane Water Electrolyzers](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c15614)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** AEM water electrolysis · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-21 · **Authors:** Kun Li, Chongao Tian, Rui Liu, Yan Guo, Feifei Shi, Ruyue Gao

Lattice-confining Ru single atoms in a Co₃O₄ spinel support shortens Co–O and Ru–O bonds to form Ru–O–Co bridges, cutting Co dissolution roughly 9-fold versus surface-coordinated Ru while lowering OER overpotential to 260 mV at 10 mA cm⁻². The resulting AEMWE ran for over 1000 h with a degradation rate of 88.1 μV h⁻¹. The authors attribute the stability gain to suppressed Co leaching and the activity gain to faster proton-coupled electron transfer via a partially activated lattice-oxygen pathway.

### [Water Activation as a Limiting Factor for High Energy Efficiency in CO2 Electroreduction](https://doi.org/10.1021/acselectrochem.6c00222)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electroreduction · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-09-29 · **Authors:** José Guillermo Rivera de la Cruz, Jonathan Raisin, Marc Fontecave, Dong Un Lee, Thomas Francisco Jaramillo, Tiras Y. Lin

Interfacial proton depletion under industrial current densities imposes a net thermodynamic voltage penalty of ~0.6 V in zero-gap MEA CO2 electrolyzers, setting a practical energy floor for C2+ production even after accounting for the pH-gradient Nernstian offset. The authors argue this penalty can only be broken by synchronizing interfacial proton delivery with dimerization kinetics, a link not yet demonstrated at high rates in acidic COR. Until then, they conclude a sequential CO2-to-CO then CO-to-C2+ cascade is the more robust industrial route.
