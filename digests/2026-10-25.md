### [Water Activation as a Limiting Factor for High Energy Efficiency in CO2 Electroreduction](https://doi.org/10.1021/acselectrochem.6c00222)

Interfacial proton depletion under industrial current densities imposes a net thermodynamic voltage penalty of ~0.6 V in zero-gap MEA CO2 electrolyzers, setting a practical energy floor for C2+ production even after accounting for the pH-gradient Nernstian offset. ### [Unraveling Water Balances behind Salt Formation in CO2 Reduction Electrolyzers](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c12691)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electrolyzer salt · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-23 · **Authors:** Yuge Feng, Shaoyun Hao, Zhou Yu, Ziteng Zuo, Tae‐Ung Wi, Ahmad Elgazzar

A quantitative water balance for CO2 electrolyzer cathodes shows that inlet CO2 through a water bubbler is often not fully humidified, so evaporation inside the cathode chamber lowers the local liquid water-to-K+ ratio and drives bicarbonate salt precipitation—even when anolyte crossover water would otherwise suffice to dissolve the K+. Excessive humidification stops salt formation but causes electrode flooding, so the work frames water management as a trade-off between dehydration-induced crystallization and over-wetting.

### [Three-Dimensionally Fibrillated Inorganic Composite Anion Exchange Membranes for Ampere-Scale Hydrogen Production](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02666)

A free-standing anion exchange membrane made of 90 wt% layered double hydroxide in a fibrillated PTFE scaffold shows zero swelling and 0.089 Ω cm² area-specific resistance, and survives 1344 h in 1 M and 30 wt% KOH at 80 °C where commercial polymer membranes fail quickly. The main caveat is that the 1 A cm⁻² durability tests are far shorter than the 1344 h ex-situ KOH soak, so long-term ampere-scale operation remains to be shown.
