This mini-review separates four often-conflated mechanisms for magnetic-field-enhanced CO2 electroreduction—radical-pair spin interconversion, field-induced polarization, Lorentz-force magnetohydrodynamic transport, and field-free spin control (chirality-induced spin selectivity, topological states, coordination-tuned spin)—and compares catalyst families (Sn/Bi for formate, Ni for CO, Cu/chiral Ag/MOFs for C2+) by field configuration and outcome. ### [Unraveling Water Balances behind Salt Formation in CO2 Reduction Electrolyzers](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c12691)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electrolyzer salt · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-23 · **Authors:** Yuge Feng, Shaoyun Hao, Zhou Yu, Ziteng Zuo, Tae‐Ung Wi, Ahmad Elgazzar

A quantitative water balance for CO2 electrolyzer cathodes shows that inlet CO2 through a water bubbler is often not fully humidified, so evaporation inside the cathode chamber lowers the local liquid water-to-K+ ratio and drives bicarbonate salt precipitation—even when anolyte crossover supplies enough water to dissolve the cotransported K+. Over-humidifying suppresses salt formation but causes electrode flooding, a direct trade-off. The finding frames cathode water management as the governing lever for salt control and stability.
