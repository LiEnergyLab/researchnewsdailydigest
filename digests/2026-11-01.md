### [Hydrophobic modification regulates the water microenvironment at the interface of Cu-based materials to promote E-CO 2 RR](https://doi.org/10.20517/energymater.2026.202)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2RR Cu catalysts · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-30 · **Authors:** K. Li, Yanxin Chen, Hao-Yan Shi, Hai-Long Wang, Yi-Hong Wu, Xiao-Wei Wu

Self-assembling octadecanethiol on cubic Cu₂O(100) nanoparticles raises the surface contact angle from 46° to 133° and yields 45.2% Faradaic efficiency for ethylene at −250 mA·cm⁻² in 1 M KOH flow cells, with suppressed HER versus bare Cu₂O. A wet-chemical route yields ~26 nm-thick Cu nanodisks with abundant fcc/2H and fcc/4H heterophase interfaces on their side surfaces. ### [Unraveling Water Balances behind Salt Formation in CO2 Reduction Electrolyzers](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c12691)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electrolyzer salt · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-23 · **Authors:** Yuge Feng, Shaoyun Hao, Zhou Yu, Ziteng Zuo, Tae‐Ung Wi, Ahmad Elgazzar

A quantitative water balance for CO2 electrolyzer cathodes shows that inlet CO2 through a water bubbler is often not fully humidified, so evaporation inside the cathode chamber lowers the local liquid water-to-K+ ratio and drives bicarbonate salt precipitation—even when anolyte crossover supplies enough water in principle. Over-humidifying suppresses salt formation but causes electrode flooding, a direct trade-off. The finding frames cathode water management as the governing variable for salt-related stability.
