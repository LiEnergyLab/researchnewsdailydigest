### [Preprint of "Cobalt Oxidation State Controls Ir–CoOₓ Catalysts for Acidic Oxygen Evolution: From Model Surfaces to PEM Water Electrolyzers"](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22046920)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** PEM electrolyzer OER catalyst · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-08-26 · **Authors:** Tomáš Hrbek, Karel Bouzek, Michal Carda

Using model thin films, the authors show that the cobalt oxide support dictates the transformation pathway of iridium during acidic oxygen evolution: Ir on Co₃O₄ forms a stable, active (oxy)hydroxide layer, while Ir on CoO is structurally unstable. This mechanistic insight was validated in a PEM electrolyzer, where mixed Ir–CoOₓ catalysts with a Co₃O₄-like environment outperformed pure Ir and IrO₂ at low Ir loadings. The work identifies the oxide environment, not just the Ir oxidation state, as a key design lever for durable, low-iridium OER catalysts.

### [Evidence of Eley–Rideal mechanism in Cu-catalysed CO2 electroreduction in flow cells with a protective layer](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41929-026-01584-x)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electroreduction mechanism · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-07-30 · **Authors:** Qiwen Sun, Ye Yao, 银泽邦, Gong Zhang, Xiaoxia Chang, Jinlong Gong

Copper catalysts in flow-cell CO₂ electrolyzers can convert CO₂ to CO via an Eley–Rideal mechanism, where gaseous CO reacts directly with an adsorbed surface species, rather than the more commonly assumed Langmuir–Hinshelwood pathway. This finding, supported by kinetic isotope effects and surface-enhanced infrared spectroscopy, indicates that a protective layer (e.g., a hydrophobic or ionomeric coating) can alter the local reaction environment to favor this direct gaseous pathway. The work provides mechanistic clarity for designing Cu-based flow-cell systems where gas-phase reactant access to the catalyst surface is deliberately controlled.
