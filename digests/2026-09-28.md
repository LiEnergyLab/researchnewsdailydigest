### [Acidic local environments inhibit CO2 reduction catalysis on coinage metals](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv-2025-kpc2c/v4)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2RR acidic electrolytes · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-09-10 · **Authors:** Max Joshua Hülsey, Bryan Yuk-Wah Tang, Sophia Weng, Yogesh Surendranath

CO2 reduction on coinage metals in acidic electrolyte only begins once interfacial hydronium is substantially depleted, meaning a local pH swing is a prerequisite for the reaction rather than a consequence of it. Using DEMS to separate faradaic from non-faradaic CO2 consumption, the authors show that added cations act mainly to create a hydronium-depleted interfacial environment, and that promoting H2 evolution (Pt-doped Ag) lowers the CO2RR onset overpotential by ~1 V. They propose that acidic local pH favors *COOH, which fragments unproductively to CO2 and H* instead of proceeding to CO.

### [Water‐Mediated Proton Facilitation for Stable Acidic CO 2 Electrocatalysis](https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.71609)

Incorporating Ce into porous Cu nanosheets creates Cu─O─Ce sites that accelerate *CO formation and protonation, enabling multi-carbon production in acidic CO₂ electroreduction while limiting the local pH rise that normally pushes the system toward neutral/alkaline conditions. The claim rests on catalyst-level performance data; the broader stability advantage over high-current-density operation is asserted rather than demonstrated over long durations.

### [Unifying Water Oxidation Chemistry of Iridium Oxide from Aqueous Model Systems to Proton Exchange Membrane Electrolysers](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15008370/v1)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** PEM electrolyser Ir OER · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-09-06 · **Authors:** Caiwu Liang, James Murawski, Cindy Tseng, Yue Wen, Yemin Tao, Chris Zalitis

Using operando X-ray and optical spectroscopy on identical IrOx catalysts, the authors find that Nafion ionomer does not change the intrinsic redox transitions or electrophilic oxygen species responsible for O–O bond formation—it mainly affects mass transport via oxygen trapping and microbubbles. Higher average Ir oxidation states seen in membrane–electrode assemblies versus aqueous cells are attributed to greater catalyst utilization rather than different chemistry, and IrOx stays highly oxidized for hours without hydrogen crossover. The work frames catalyst utilization and transport, not reaction mechanism, as the main source of model–device discrepancies.
