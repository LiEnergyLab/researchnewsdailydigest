### [The CO2 Concentration Inversion Effect: Definition, Quantitative Mechanistic Basis, and Design Rules for Dilute CO2 Electroreduction](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15006687/v1)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electroreduction · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-07-29 · **Authors:** Qing Yu, Ying Zhang, Peng Zhu, Qiankang Zhang

Under dilute CO2 feeds (25–50%), copper-based catalysts can achieve C2+ product selectivity that matches or exceeds that seen with pure CO2, contradicting the usual assumption that lower CO2 partial pressure hurts performance. This "inversion" arises because the rate-limiting step shifts from C–C coupling to *CO supply, meaning catalyst design should balance *CO generation with coupling-site availability rather than simply maximizing coupling sites. The effect is specific to copper catalysts and its exact concentration boundaries are still being mapped.

### [Quantifying Heterogeneities at the Mesoscale via Tomography-Based Pore-Scale CO2 Electroreduction Modeling](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15006707/v1)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electroreduction · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-07-29 · **Authors:** F. Lorenzutti, E.F. Johnson, E. Boutin, Sophia Haussener

Pore-scale simulations of a silver gas diffusion electrode show that local catalyst-layer morphology and flooding state cause significant spatial variations in CO₂ concentration, pH, and current density that volume-averaged models miss. The work underscores that accounting for pore-scale variability is necessary to accurately predict and optimize eCO2R device performance.
