### [Beyond Molecular Dispersion: A Molecular-Cone Architecture of Cobalt Phthalocyanine on CNTs for Selective CO2 Electroreduction to Methanol](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.6c04116)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electroreduction · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-08-15 · **Authors:** Yi-Xiang Wang, Rui Teng, Yunshuyu Sun, Meng Zhang, Yuxiang Mao, R Y

A CNT-supported nitro-substituted cobalt phthalocyanine catalyst that forms a "molecular-cone" structure avoids the aggregation penalty at high loadings, achieving a methanol Faradaic efficiency of 64.4% at –0.98 V vs RHE and retaining 58.4% at 150 mA cm⁻² in a membrane electrode assembly. The cone geometry enhances local electric fields and K⁺ enrichment, which stabilizes the *CO intermediate and reorganizes interfacial water to speed up proton transfer and hydrogenation kinetics. This work offers a microenvironment-engineering route to boost both methanol selectivity and current density for CO₂ electroreduction.

### [Electrolyser and anaerobic digestion driven CO2 hydrogenation routes towards sustainable dimethyl ether production: A review of carbon capture technologies, advanced reactor technologies, and sustainability assessment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nxener.2026.100902)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 utilisation · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-08-15 · **Authors:** Avinash Ankush Lad, Aniket Y. Patrikar, Ratnadip R. Joshi

This review compares two renewable dimethyl ether (DME) production routes—one powered by water-splitting electrolysis and the other by anaerobic digestion—and finds that neither is inherently superior. The environmental and economic performance of each route is highly conditional, depending on factors like electricity source, feedstock, and life-cycle assessment boundaries. The electrolyser route can achieve a low carbon footprint only with low-carbon electricity, while the digestion route can be net-negative under specific waste-management scenarios, but both require clearly defined assumptions to make such claims.

### [Where to address N2 and reactive impurities in the CO2 electrochemical value chain?](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joule.2026.102626)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electrolysis impurities · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-08-01 · **Authors:** Laura Gatti, Alessio Mezza, Adriano Sacco, Thomas Burdyny

The paper argues that making CO2 electrolyzers tolerant to dilute or impure feeds just shifts the separation problem downstream, where it can be harder and more hazardous. Specifically, downstream CO/N2 separation is more difficult than upstream CO2/N2 separation, and post-electrolysis O2 can create flammability risks with H2 byproduct. ### [Evidence of Eley–Rideal mechanism in Cu-catalysed CO2 electroreduction in flow cells with a protective layer](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41929-026-01584-x)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electroreduction · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-07-30 · **Authors:** Qiwen Sun, Ye Yao, 银泽邦, Gong Zhang, Xiaoxia Chang, Jinlong Gong

Copper catalysts in flow-cell CO₂ electrolyzers can reduce CO₂ via the Eley–Rideal mechanism, where gaseous CO reacts directly with an adsorbed intermediate, rather than only through the traditionally assumed Langmuir–Hinshelwood pathway. This finding, supported by kinetic isotope effects and surface-enhanced infrared spectroscopy, implies that gas-phase transport and local CO concentration near the catalyst surface are more influential in determining product selectivity than previously thought. The protective layer used in the flow cell appears to stabilize this pathway by maintaining a favorable local environment.

**Score:** 7/10 · **Tag:** Fuel cell ionomer inks · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-08-09 · **Authors:** Siddharth Rajupet, Niki Jacquin, Sarah A. Berlinger, Maximilian Degirmenci, Finn Babbe, Nathan Stovall

PFSA ionomer adsorption onto catalyst particles in inks is electrostatically self-limiting, with adsorption ceasing once the particle surface reaches a common negative charge density (around a 25 kBT energy barrier), regardless of particle type. ### [Multidimensional Design of Metal-Nitrogen-Carbon Electrocatalysts for Direct Propylene Epoxidation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14351v1)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Tag:** electrochemical epoxidation · **Journal:** arXiv · **Published:** 2026-08-14 · **Authors:** Songbo Ye, Qingyuan Han, Jingwen Chi, Yuan Huang, Heng Liu, Di Zhang, Hitoshi Shiku, Li Wei, Hao Li

Propylene epoxidation selectivity in metal-nitrogen-carbon catalysts is governed by a combination of oxygen binding energy, the potential of zero charge, and applied potential—not by oxygen binding alone. ### [Electrostatic Phenomenology Benchmarks for Machine-Learned Interatomic Potentials in Electrochemistry: Beyond the Energy-Force Metric](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14153v1)

Cucinotta, Richard G. Hennig, Karsten Reuter, Stefan Ringe, Mira Todorova, Christoph Freysoldt, Jörg Neugebauer

The standard practice of validating machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) solely on aggregate energy and force errors is insufficient for electrochemistry, as it misses critical qualitative failures like incorrect image-charge attraction or dielectric screening.
