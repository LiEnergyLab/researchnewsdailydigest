### [Unraveling Water Balances behind Salt Formation in CO2 Reduction Electrolyzers](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c12691)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2-electrolyzer · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-23 · **Authors:** Yuge Feng, Shaoyun Hao, Zhou Yu, Ziteng Zuo, Tae‐Ung Wi, Ahmad Elgazzar

A quantitative water balance for CO2 electrolyzer cathodes shows that inlet CO2 through a water bubbler is often not fully humidified, so evaporation inside the cathode chamber lowers the local liquid water-to-K+ ratio and drives bicarbonate salt precipitation—even when anolyte crossover supplies enough water to dissolve the cotransported K+. A free-standing anion exchange membrane made of 90 wt% layered double hydroxide in a fibrillated PTFE scaffold shows zero swelling and 0.089 Ω cm² area-specific resistance, and it survived 1,344 h in 1 M and 30 wt% KOH at 80 °C where commercial polymer membranes fail quickly. ### [Atomistic Simulations Facilitating the Synthesis of the Desired Fe–Cu–N–C Dual-Atomic Site for Acidic Oxygen Reduction in Fuel Cells](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsnano.6c06911)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** PEMFC-catalysts · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-05 · **Authors:** Md Raziun Bin Mamtaz, Frédéric Jaouen, Jun Chen, Chen Jia, Quentin P.-G. Meyer, Chuan Zhao

DFT and XAS together show that the desired Fe–Cu–N–C dual-atom site is an Fe–Cu bond in an N4 coordination environment, and that forming metal-vacant N–C ligands during synthesis is the key step to obtaining it. The result is a synthesis rationale for controlling local coordination in dual-atom catalysts rather than a heuristic trial-and-error approach.
