### [Competing Surface Dynamics of Copper Catalyst Particles during Electrochemical Carbon Dioxide Reduction](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsnano.6c11607)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2RR Cu catalysts · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-11-12 · **Authors:** Yifan Chen, Tom Burwell, Madasamy Thangamuthu, Sadegh Ghaderzadeh, Gazi N. Aliev, Haytham E. M. Hussein

Using identical-location TEM on individual Cu nanoparticles on graphitized carbon nanofibers, the authors observed Ostwald ripening, coalescence, selective etching, and charge-driven fragmentation coexisting in the same region under CO2RR, with fragmentation seen in particles >10 nm. Solution-mediated dissolution–precipitation dominated at open-circuit potential and degraded the catalyst before reaction, while under CO2RR surface restructuring was sensitive to support nanotexture and defects. The work maps competing surface-confined restructuring pathways relevant to Cu catalyst stability.

### [Cu-Doped Highly Porous SnO2 for Long-Lasting Acidic CO2 Electroreduction at 1000 mA cm–2](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02917)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** acidic CO2RR SnO2 · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-11-12 · **Authors:** Kaili Zhu, Bingquan Jia, Licheng Sun, Biaobiao Zhang

Cu-doped porous SnO2 sustains 1000 mA cm–2 for 19 h at 98.5% Faradaic efficiency for formic acid in pH 1.82 acidic flow-cell CO2 reduction, and 800 mA cm–2 for 72 h at 94.1% FE. ### [Balancing CO2 Mass Transport and Salt Precipitation via Multiparameter Optimization for Efficient Syngas Production in MEA Electrolyzers](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.6c04109)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** MEA CO2 electrolyzer · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-11-12 · **Authors:** Pan Huang, Shaojie Zheng, Xuefeng He, Hao Lei, Zhenting Xie, Qin Peng

A multiparameter sweep of catalyst loading, GDL wettability, CO2 flow/temperature, and acid-vapor humidification identified an optimum (1.0 mg cm⁻² loading, 154° contact angle, 100 sccm, 50 °C inlet, 0.1 M HCl humidification) that balanced CO2 transport against salt precipitation in an MEA CO2 electrolyzer. The result is a practical operating-window recipe rather than a new catalyst or mechanism.

### [Unraveling Water Balances behind Salt Formation in CO2 Reduction Electrolyzers](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c12691)
**Score:** 10/10 · **Tag:** CO2 electrolyzer salt/water balance · **Journal:** OpenAlex · **Published:** 2026-10-23 · **Authors:** Yuge Feng, Shaoyun Hao, Zhou Yu, Ziteng Zuo, Tae‐Ung Wi, Ahmad Elgazzar

A quantitative water balance for CO2 electrolyzer cathodes shows that inlet CO2 from a water bubbler is often not fully humidified, so evaporation inside the cathode chamber lowers the local liquid water-to-K+ ratio and drives bicarbonate salt precipitation—even though anolyte crossover water would otherwise suffice to dissolve the cotransported K+. Over-humidifying suppresses salt formation but causes electrode flooding, so the authors frame cathode water activity as the governing trade-off variable for stability. The finding is a mass-balance/engineering analysis rather than a new material or device demonstration.
